## 2. Threat model, applied to this product

Framing: STRIDE categories, but only failure modes this repository evidences. A children's-safety product inverts the usual priority: **the adversary inside the trust boundary is a minor the product is designed to constrain, and the asset is that child's location, communications and device.** Every row below names the evidence that makes it a real threat rather than a checklist item.

| # | Threat (STRIDE) | Scenario in this product | Why the current design does not stop it | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TM-1 | **Elevation of privilege** — child escalates against their own controls | A child holding their own valid JWT calls `device-register` again with a self-chosen `secret_hash` | `secret_hash` is **taken from the client** (`readHex64(secret_hash)`) and written on insert/update; the family/child binding is checked but the credential material is not server-minted. The child can therefore re-key their own device row and drive whatever client holds the matching secret | `capture-20261030.json:80-82`; `14:230` (E10); `16-phase-e-findings.md:387` |
| TM-2 | **Spoofing** — silent control of a child's phone via a leaked device secret | `x-device-id` + `x-device-secret` are the *entire* credential for `device-telemetry` (all four actions) and for `device-command` poll/ack | The device channel needs no user JWT at all, so a leaked header pair grants command pickup and telemetry writes indefinitely. It is protected by a constant-time comparison and by `credential_revoked_at` — **the only per-credential revocation primitive in the system** — but nothing revokes it on the parent's action set except `family-admin.revoke_device` | `capture:64,105`; `14:155` |
| TM-3 | **Repudiation** — a maintenance actor leaves no trace | `cron` expires every stale remote session and flips devices offline, authenticated by one header | `cron` inlines **only `cors` and `envelope`** — neither `audit` nor `auth` nor `rate_limit`.
